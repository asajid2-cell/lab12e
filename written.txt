Q3. What happens to the bounding boxes with update + fullRefit?

fullRefit only recomputes each box from the circles that are already assigned to that node, so
the circles never change parent. As they wander, a circle can end up sitting well outside its
own node's box, and because parent boxes are just the union of their children, the error
compounds all the way up. Every frame the boxes only grow: they never shrink back when a circle
moves away, and a child that drifts out to the far side of its parent stretches that parent's box
to include it. My measurement on the 100-circle scene: total perimeter of the internal boxes is
3.3x bigger than a freshly built tree after 600 frames, growing steadily (1.0x, 1.7x, 2.3x,
2.8x, 2.9x, 3.2x, 3.3x at frames 0, 100, ... 600). Bigger boxes overlap more, so fewer subtrees
get rejected and the ray test visits more circles per pixel. The picture also starts to look
wrong, because the root box quickly stops matching where the circles actually are. Refitting is
cheap though - about 0.4 microseconds per frame - so the cost is entirely in the degraded tree,
not in the repair.

Q4. What happens with reset (rebuild every frame) instead?

The tree is thrown away and rebuilt from the current positions every frame, so the boxes are
always tight and the tree always reflects the current arrangement. Every internal box in the
rebuilt tree matches a from-scratch build exactly (I checked this by rebuilding twice and
comparing every node), and query results matched brute force at every sampled pixel before and
after the reset. The price is rebuilding: about 81 microseconds per frame in my measurement,
roughly 200x the cost of a refit. That is still small next to the nearly 800,000 pixel samples
each frame, which is why the program still runs at a reasonable rate. The real trade-off is that
reset throws away a structure that was still mostly usable just to correct a drift that, at these
speeds, is only a few pixels per frame.

Q5. Why are some circles drawn with their sides cut off?

Two separate things cut them off. The first is the window edge: circles are created anywhere in
[0, width] x [0, height] with a radius up to 20, so a circle whose centre lands within a radius
of the border is partly outside the image no matter what, and nothing in the ray test can paint
pixels that are not on the screen. The second, which is the one that shows up once the circles
move, is that update() just adds the velocity with no walls, so after a while many circles have
drifted completely past the edge and only a sliver of them, or none, is left in view. In my run
66 of the 100 circles had crossed the edge after 340 frames, one by as much as 671 pixels. On top
of both of those, circles overlap and the ray test keeps the colour of the last circle it hits,
so a circle can also be hidden by one drawn on top of it - that is a circle being covered, not
cut off, but it looks the same in a still frame.
